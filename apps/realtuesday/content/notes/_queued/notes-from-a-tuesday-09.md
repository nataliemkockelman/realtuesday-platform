## the confession.

People ask about my setup expecting a list of apps. What I actually run is almost embarrassingly plain, and the punchline is that most of it isn't about email at all.

The inbox is just the place where other people's requests happen to land. Fixing the inbox means fixing what happens to a request, not learning to sort mail faster.

## the reframe.

Here's what most inbox advice misses. A message in your inbox is not information. It's a piece of work in disguise, wearing a polite outfit. "Quick question," "just following up," "can you send me," every one of them is a task that hasn't been named yet.

So my whole system has one job: turn each incoming request into a decision, a task somewhere I trust, or a reply, and then get it out of the inbox. The inbox is a doormat, not a filing cabinet. Nothing lives there.

> Your inbox isn't a list of messages. It's a pile of other people's unfinished tasks, and treating it like reading material is why it never ends.

## what's actually in the setup.

The unglamorous version. A tiny number of folders, because elaborate folder trees are a way to feel organized while doing nothing. Search does the finding, not filing.

A hard rule that a message either gets handled in one pass or becomes a task with a real home, never left half-read to "deal with later," because later is where inboxes go to rot.

A handful of templates for the answers I write over and over, so the ninetieth version of the same reply takes seconds instead of pretending to be original.

And yes, some of the sorting and drafting is done by AI now. But I only pointed the AI at the inbox after the process was already sane. Automating a mess just hands you a faster mess with a subscription attached. The tool is the last tenth of it. The habit is the rest.

## why it matters.

An inbox is where good intentions go to drown. Not because there's too much mail, but because most people treat each message as something to look at instead of something to move.

Fix what happens to a request and the volume stops mattering. That's the whole trick, and it holds whether you get thirty messages a day or three hundred. It was never about email.
